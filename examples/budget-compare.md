# Example: Budget Comparison

## Prompt

```
"Compare gaming laptops at $1000, $1500, and $2000 price points"
```

## What Happens

1. **Agent identifies** three budget tiers
2. **Researches** best options at each price
3. **Compares** what you get at each tier

## Expected Response

```markdown

## $1,000 Tier - Entry Gaming

### ASUS TUF Gaming A15 - $999.99
**Buy**: [Amazon](https://amazon.com/...)

**Specs**:
- AMD Ryzen 5 7535HS

- 15.6" 144Hz display

**Gaming Performance**:
- 1080p Medium: 60-90 FPS (AAA games)
- 1080p High: 45-60 FPS
- Esports titles: 100+ FPS

**Trade-offs at this price**:
- Plastic build quality
- Average display color accuracy
- Louder fans under load

## $1,500 Tier - Sweet Spot

**Buy**: [Best Buy](https://bestbuy.com/...)

- AMD Ryzen 7 7745HX

**What $500 more gets you**:
- ✅ 40% better GPU performance
- ✅ QHD display (sharper image)
- ✅ Metal build, better cooling
- ✅ Double the storage

## $2,000 Tier - High-End

### ASUS ROG Strix G16 - $1,999.99
**Buy**: [Best Buy](https://bestbuy.com/...)

**Specs**:
- Intel Core i9-13980HX
- NVIDIA RTX 4070 (8GB)

- 16" 240Hz QHD display

**Gaming Performance**:

- ✅ 32GB RAM (future-proof, content creation)

## Recommendation

**Best value**: $1,500 tier (Legion 5 Pro)
- Biggest jump in capability vs. entry tier
- Handles 1440p gaming well
- Good enough for most users

**Skip the $2,000 tier if**:
- You only game at 1080p
- You don't need 32GB RAM
- You're not into competitive esports

**Worth $2,000 if**:
- You want max settings + ray tracing
- You do video editing / 3D work
- You want the laptop to last 4+ years
```

## Tips

- **Specify exact budgets**: Makes comparison more useful
- **Mention your games**: "I play Cyberpunk" vs "I play Valorant"
- **State your resolution**: 1080p, 1440p, or 4K
